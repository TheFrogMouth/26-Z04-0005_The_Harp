\chapter{Hardware Architecture}\label{ch:architecture}

\section{Why This Hardware}
The Harp reuses two existing designs. The digital and audio chain is the Timekeeper's DSP board (25-Z01-0001, \texttt{kicad/dsp\_board}); the board outline, face, jacks, pots and relay bypass are The Relic's, which in turn follow The Alchemist.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Same codec, flash, buck converter, input and output stages and relay bypass} as the Timekeeper and The Relic, so most of the schematic is copied.
  \item \textbf{No SDRAM.} The strings need about 100\,KB of delay memory and the STM32H750 has 1\,MB of internal RAM. Dropping SDRAM removes the external memory bus and about 60 MCU pins.
  \item \textbf{STM32H750VBT6 in LQFP-100} instead of the Timekeeper's LQFP-144: the same core and firmware with a new pin map.
  \item \textbf{No TFT.} Twelve note LEDs replace the screen.
  \item \textbf{Pots, not encoders.} Encoders make sense only with a screen that shows the value. Pots also keep The Relic's face, parts and feel, and the H750 reads them on ADC1 PA0--PA5, the six-channel DMA scan that the Timekeeper's \texttt{controls.c} already runs.
  \item \textbf{Jacks and DC jack at The Alchemist's positions and heights.} This is the main reason the design is one board (Chapter~\ref{ch:pcb}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Bypass Circuit}
The bypass is The Relic's circuit with The Alchemist's relay. An Omron G6K-2F-Y (DC5, 5.2\,mm tall, SMD) is driven by a 2N7002 from the MCU. The 5\,V coil is fed from $+9$\,V through 150\,$\Omega$, and a pull-down holds the relay off through boot. Pole~A switches OUT~L between IN and the DSP output; pole~B disconnects the input buffer from IN and grounds it. The firmware keeps the relay drive low from reset until the codec is running and its outputs are silent, so a pedal that fails to start still passes the guitar to OUT~L.
