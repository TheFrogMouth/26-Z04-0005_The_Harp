\chapter{Schematic Design}\label{ch:schematic}

\section{Sheets}
The schematic is a root sheet plus seven sheets in \texttt{kicad/the\_harp/}. They were generated by \texttt{scripts/harp\_schematic/build\_layout.py} from \texttt{harp\_spec.py}. The circuits are the Timekeeper's unless noted; the bypass is The Relic's. Every pin carries a label or power symbol on a short stub (The Relic's convention). The generator re-reads its own output and traces every net against the specification: no shorts, no single-pin nets, every pin assigned. It reports 190 parts, 49 global nets and no problems. The sheets have not yet been opened in KiCad, and ERC has not been run.

\section{Differences from the Timekeeper}
All of these are deliberate.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Stereo in.} Two input buffers and two ADC-driver channels; the codec's AINL and AINR are both used and nothing is parked. A mono plug grounds the IN ring and the firmware copies left to right.
  \item \textbf{ADC-driver reference.} The Timekeeper left the driver's IN\_N open. Here it goes to VCOM\_A, the DC level of the input buffer output; this is the fix recommended by the Timekeeper's input noise review (finding H1). The THS4522 stays: it is made for the single 5\,V analogue rail, with a rail-to-rail output and VOCM pinning the output common mode to the codec's VCOM. Replacing it with the spare OPA1656 half was tried and reverted on 2026-10-05.
  \item \textbf{Input op-amp OPA2365, not OPA1656} (Section~\ref{sec:inputstage}).
  \item \textbf{H2 fix.} \req{R705} (the Timekeeper's R810) is 49.9\,$\Omega$ rather than 5.1\,k$\Omega$, and \req{R602} is 1.05\,k$\Omega$ to match the signal leg, so the legs are balanced and the gain is no longer cut to about 0.16.
  \item \textbf{Gain 0.59, not 1.} At unity a hard-played humbucker already reaches ADC full scale and a synth or line source clips.
  \item \textbf{No input pad} (decision 11).
  \item \textbf{Outputs not crossed.} The Timekeeper wired AOUTR to its left output; here AOUTL is OUT~L. The channels are swapped in firmware when porting.
  \item \textbf{Output pull-downs.} 100\,$\Omega$ in series and 1\,M$\Omega$ to GND after each 10\,\textmu F (The Relic's convention), so the outputs sit at 0\,V when the DSP is muted.
  \item \textbf{QUADSPI on bank~2} (Section~\ref{sec:pinmap}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Input Stage}\label{sec:inputstage}
The Timekeeper's OPA1656 has an input range of V$-$ to V$+$ minus 2.25\,V (datasheet SBOS901C, section 6.6). On the 5\,V rail that stops at 2.75\,V, only 0.25\,V above the 2.5\,V bias. The Timekeeper's bench figures (input noise review, H2 table: peaks at $-16$\,dBFS with a gain of 0.152) put hard-played peaks at about $\pm$1.5\,V at the buffer, so positive peaks run more than 1\,V past that limit. The OPA2365 is a pin-compatible, zero-crossover, rail-to-rail-input part for 2.2 to 5.5\,V, so the 2.5\,V bias, the THS4522 and the 5\,V rail stay as they are. A 9\,V buffer supply was ruled out. The OPA2156 was rejected because its two input stages would put every positive peak across their handover step.

The Harp's input stage is the Timekeeper's, with the same values (\req{U701} = U801, \req{R705} = R810, \req{R601}/\req{R602} = R501/R502, \req{C702} = C807). The Timekeeper's input-chain simulation (\texttt{25-Z01-0001\_DSP\_Development\_Board/simulations/InputChain}) therefore covers it:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Stability} into about 320\,pF through 49.9\,$\Omega$. The OPA2365 data sheet gives unity-gain stability to about 1\,nF and recommends 10 to 20\,$\Omega$ isolation. Simulated overshoot at the THS4522 input is at most 0.5\,\% for open-loop output resistances of 10 to 100\,$\Omega$, against 42 to 76\,\% without the resistor.
  \item \textbf{Noise} about 11\,nV/$\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ at 1\,kHz, estimated from the data sheet's 100\,kHz and 0.1--10\,Hz figures. It adds about 0.9\,dB over the pickup and bias-resistor noise at the ADC. The 1\,kHz figure is still to be confirmed on the data sheet plot.
  \item \textbf{Gain and headroom.} The gain from the jack to the ADC is 0.59 with \req{R603}/\req{R604} at 620\,$\Omega$ (0.95 at 1\,k$\Omega$). A 4.8\,Vpp input, the most the OPA2365 can swing on 5\,V, just reaches full scale. A normally played single coil lands near $-18$\,dBFS, a hard-played humbucker near $-4$\,dBFS and a $+4$\,dBu synth near $-3$\,dBFS.
\end{itemize}
