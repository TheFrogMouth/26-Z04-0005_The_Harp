\chapter{PCB and Mechanical Design}\label{ch:pcb}

\section{One Board or Two}
The jacks decide it. The Alchemist and The Relic mount the four 1/4-inch jacks and the DC jack on the underside of the pot board, so the wall-hole heights are set by the board's depth under the face (about 10 to 12\,mm on the pot bushings) plus the jack's axis height. To put the Harp's jacks at the same positions and heights, they must sit on a board hanging at that same depth, and that is the board the pots are on.

\begin{description}
  \item[Plan A, one board (default).] The Alchemist and Relic 56 by 90\,mm outline with the DC tab and four layers, carrying the pots, toggles, LEDs, jacks, relay, analogue section and the whole digital section.
  \item[Plan B, fallback.] A face board keeps everything that touches the enclosure: pots, toggles, LEDs, jacks, DC, relay and the input and output buffers. The H750, flash, codec and power move to a DSP board below it, joined by a 24-way 1.0\,mm FFC as the Timekeeper's two boards are. The face board keeps The Relic's outline exactly, so jacks and pots do not move. The cost is a second PCB, two FFC connectors and the cable (about 3\,EUR at 100) and about 8\,mm more depth, which the 125B has.
\end{description}

The Relic places 120 parts on this outline with room to spare in the digital zone. The Timekeeper's DSP board carries 145 parts, including SDRAM and a 144-pin MCU, on a larger board. About 190 parts, most of them 0402 and 0603 with a 14 by 14\,mm LQFP-100, on 56 by 90\,mm less the DC tab is tight but expected to fit. SMD parts may sit over the jack bodies (The Relic's rule), and the four-layer stack keeps routing off the top layer. The go/no-go is placement: if the codec and MCU groups cannot be zoned away from the input buffer with the LEDs in place, the design moves to Plan B rather than being squeezed.

\section{Enclosure and Datum}
Taken unchanged from The Relic:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tayda 125B: face 64.7 by 119.8\,mm, cavity 60.3 by 115.4\,mm, corner bosses avoided. These are nominal figures until a casting is measured.
  \item Datum: the enclosure centre is PCB (148.5, 105), face X right, face Y up; the drill/place origin and grid origin are there.
  \item Board: 56 by 90\,mm, corner radius 2\,mm, face X $\pm$28, Y $+52$ to $-38$, with an 18\,mm tab to $+56.5$ under the DC jack. Components on the top side only, except the jacks. Through-hole parts only where no jack body is underneath; SMD parts over the jack bodies are allowed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Controls over the Upper Jacks}\label{sec:toggleconflict}
The upper jack bodies sit on the underside at face X $\pm$6.5 to $\pm$30, Y $+5.6$ to $-12.6$, with their rear pin row at Y $+4.6$. The first face, The Relic's six knobs and three toggles, did not fit them: the outer lower-row pots have their pins at Y $+5.5$, on the jacks' rear pads, and the outer toggles at ($\pm$20, $-5$) put their pins over the jack bodies. The Relic has no upper jacks, and The Alchemist puts toggles, not pots, at ($\pm$20, $+13$), so neither board meets this.

The Harp now uses The Alchemist's arrangement (decision 13). The outer positions of the $+13$ row carry the Tuning and Brightness toggles; a toggle's pins sit around its bat, from Y $+8.3$ up, 1.3\,mm clear of the jack bodies. Jawari is the only knob in that row, at X 0 where there is no jack body. The Retune toggle at (0, $-5$) uses the Taiway footprint, whose pins lie within X $\pm$2.4, about 2.7\,mm clear of the bodies. The board has no through-hole pad over a jack.

\section{OLED Mounting}
The 0.91\,in module (envelope 36--38\,mm by 12--12.5\,mm, active area 22.384\,mm by 5.584\,mm) sits behind a 24.4\,mm by 7.6\,mm rectangular cut centred at face (0, $-5$), a Tayda rectangular cut that leaves a 1\,mm border round the active area. It moved there from (0, $-20$) on 2026-10-09 (option 1, decision 16): in the middle of the face, under the Jawari knob and away from the footswitches. The Retune toggle that stood there moved down to (0, $-24$); the module spans Y $+1.25$ to $-11.25$, 5.7\,mm clear of the toggle's body.

The module hangs from the face, not the PCB: a 3D-printed carrier, as the Timekeeper's TFT frame, is bonded to the inside of the face with VHB tape, with a 0.5\,mm foam gasket round the glass. It is not screwed to the board because the lower jack bodies sit under the board at $|X| > 6.5$ and the MCU fills the middle. Four wires run from the module pads to \req{J408}, a JST SH 4-way on the top side at face (22, $-3.5$), between the right upper jack's pin rows. The module is mounted turned round, pads at its right end, and the firmware flips the image.

The module hangs about 4.15\,mm below the face, leaving 6.85\,mm to the PCB at the provisional 11\,mm depth. Only flat parts sit under the module after the placement: \req{U201} (1.6\,mm, 5.25\,mm clear), its decoupling and part of the codec. The relay \req{K401} (5.2\,mm) and the electrolytics are kept out of the module outline. The drawings are in \texttt{docs/images/oled-face-cutout.png} and \texttt{docs/images/oled-pcb-relation.png}.

\section{Zoning}
Signal runs right to left as on The Alchemist: IN and EXP enter on the right wall, OUT~L and OUT~R leave on the left. After option 1 (2026-10-09) the H750 sits in the middle under the OLED, the codec to its left next to the SAI pins, the ADC driver below the codec, the input buffers and the flash on the right next to IN, and the output stages between the pot rows. The buck converter and the LDO sit in the top band either side of the DC jack.

\section{Placement}
All 181 footprints were placed by script (\texttt{scripts/pcb\_placement/}; drawing in \texttt{docs/images/pcb-placement.png}) on both sides, on a 0.5\,mm grid with like parts lined up in each group. The check from the board file finds no courtyard overlaps on either side apart from the stacked jacks, nothing on a through-hole from the other side, everything inside the outline and nothing tall under the OLED module.

With option 1 the H750 (17.5\,mm courtyard) moved up to face (0, $-7$), under the screen, once the Retune toggle left the middle. The codec sits beside it, next to the SAI pins (11--16\,mm), with the ADC driver below it (left-channel ADC pair 17--19\,mm). The input buffers and the flash sit on the right next to IN, the output stages between the pot rows. Thirty-seven parts that gain nothing from the top are on the bottom: the relay and its driver under U201, the LED and footswitch drivers, the expression buffer, the pot RC filters, and the SWD and footswitch pads. The cost of option 1 is a longer IN run to the relay (44\,mm) and the buffered input crossing under the Retune toggle to the ADC driver; both are routable.

Bottom-side parts need double-sided assembly or hand fitting (0603/0805, SOT-23 and the SMD relay); the SWD and footswitch pads are bare copper. Plan~A fits.

\section{Layout Rules}
Carried over from The Relic:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Four layers: F.Cu for signals and parts, In1.Cu solid GND that is never cut, In2.Cu for power ($+$9V, $+$3V3, $+$3V3A, $+$5VA), B.Cu for longer signals and the jacks.
  \item Every SMD GND pad gets its own via to In1.Cu; through-hole parts use their barrels. No via under the SWD needle pads.
  \item Analogue and digital are separated by placement, not by a split plane. The codec's analogue side and the input and output buffers face the jacks; the MCU, flash and buck stay in the centre and at the DC end. QSPI, SAI and SWD tracks stay inside the MCU zone.
  \item Parts on both sides since 2026-10-08; the bottom only where the jack bodies are not.
  \item The buck's switching node is kept off B.Cu over the jack bodies.
  \item DRC uses The Relic's \texttt{.kicad\_dru}.
\end{itemize}
